# TOTAL QUALITY MANAGEMENT (TQM)


## 1. Introduction to Total Quality Management (TQM)

Total Quality Management (TQM) is a management approach used by an organization to improve the quality of its products, services, and processes continuously.

The main aim of TQM is to provide maximum customer satisfaction by producing high-quality products with minimum defects and waste.

In simple words:

TQM means that everyone in an organization, from top-level management to workers, is responsible for maintaining and improving quality.

It focuses on:

- Producing good-quality products.
- Satisfying customer needs.
- Reducing errors and defects.
- Improving production processes.
- Reducing wastage and production costs.
- Encouraging teamwork.
- Continuously improving the organization.

### Simple example

Consider a mobile phone manufacturing company.

A mobile phone contains several components:

- Battery
- Display
- Camera
- Speaker
- Processor
- Circuit board

If the company produces a phone with a defective battery or damaged display, the customer will not be satisfied.

By implementing TQM:

1. The company checks the quality of raw materials.
2. Workers carefully assemble the components.
3. Engineers test the mobile phone.
4. Defects are identified and corrected.
5. Customer feedback is collected.
6. The company improves the manufacturing process.

As a result, the company produces better-quality mobile phones and increases customer satisfaction.

Therefore, TQM involves improving quality at every stage of production.

## 2. Definition of TQM

Total Quality Management can be defined as:

"Total Quality Management is an organization-wide management approach that focuses on continuous improvement of products, services, and processes through the participation of all employees to achieve customer satisfaction."

The three important words in TQM are:

| Word       | Meaning                                                             |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Total      | Everyone in the organization participates.                          |
| Quality    | Producing products and services according to customer requirements. |
| Management | Planning, controlling, and improving activities.                    |

Remember: TQM = Everyone + Quality + Continuous Improvement.

## 3. Objectives of Total Quality Management

The major objectives of TQM are explained below.

### 1. Customer Satisfaction

The main objective of TQM is to satisfy customers by providing products and services that meet their expectations.

Example: A mobile company produces smartphones with good battery life, display quality, and performance.

### 2. Continuous Improvement

TQM encourages organizations to improve their products, services, and processes continuously.

Example: A company regularly improves its manufacturing process to reduce defects.

### 3. Reduction of Defects

TQM aims to identify and eliminate errors during production.

Example: Testing electronic circuit boards before installing them in a product.

### 4. Reduction of Production Cost

By reducing defects, wastage, and unnecessary activities, the organization can reduce production costs.

### 5. Employee Participation

Every employee should participate in quality improvement activities.

Example: Workers suggest ideas to improve the production process.

### 6. Improvement in Productivity

TQM helps employees and machines work efficiently, increasing the number of good-quality products produced.

### 7. Better Supplier Relationships

TQM encourages organizations to select reliable suppliers who provide good-quality raw materials.

### 8. Maintaining High Standards

TQM ensures that products and services consistently meet the required quality standards.

## 4. Principles of Total Quality Management

There are eight important principles of TQM.



### 1. Customer Focus

Customers are the most important part of any business.

An organization should understand customer needs and expectations and provide products that satisfy them.

Example: A laptop manufacturing company collects customer feedback to improve battery life and processing speed.

Importance:

- Increases customer satisfaction.
- Builds customer trust.
- Improves product quality.
- Increases business growth.

### 2. Leadership

Leadership means that top-level management should guide and motivate employees to achieve quality objectives.

Managers must create a quality-focused working environment.

Example: A production manager guides workers to follow safety and quality procedures during manufacturing.

Importance:

- Provides clear direction.
- Motivates employees.
- Establishes quality objectives.
- Encourages teamwork.

### 3. Employee Involvement

Every employee should participate in quality improvement activities.

Quality is not only the responsibility of managers. Workers, engineers, technicians, and supervisors must work together.

Example: An electronics technician identifies a problem in PCB soldering and suggests a better soldering method.

Importance:

- Improves teamwork.
- Develops employee skills.
- Reduces mistakes.
- Encourages new ideas.

### 4. Process Approach

A process is a series of activities used to convert raw materials into finished products.

TQM focuses on improving each process to obtain better results.

Example: In mobile phone manufacturing:

Raw material → PCB assembly → Component installation → Testing → Packaging → Finished product.

Each stage must be properly controlled to maintain quality.

### 5. Continuous Improvement

Continuous improvement means regularly improving products, services, and working methods.

It is also called Kaizen, a Japanese concept meaning continuous improvement.

Example: A company continuously improves its PCB manufacturing process to reduce soldering defects.

Benefits:

- Improves product quality.
- Reduces wastage.
- Increases productivity.
- Reduces manufacturing costs.

### 6. Evidence-Based Decision Making

Decisions should be made using facts, measurements, and data rather than assumptions.

Example: A company records the number of defective products every day and analyzes the data to identify the cause of defects.

Benefits:

- Provides accurate decisions.
- Identifies problems.
- Reduces risks.
- Improves quality control.

### 7. Relationship Management

An organization should maintain good relationships with suppliers, customers, employees, and business partners.

Example: A mobile phone manufacturing company works closely with battery suppliers to ensure that batteries meet quality requirements.

Benefits:

- Improves supplier quality.
- Builds trust.
- Ensures continuous material supply.
- Improves business performance.

### 8. System Approach to Management

All departments in an organization should work together as one system to achieve common quality objectives.

Example: The design, production, testing, purchasing, and sales departments coordinate with one another to manufacture a quality product.

## 5. Important Elements of TQM

The major elements of Total Quality Management are:

1. Customer satisfaction
2. Top management commitment
3. Employee involvement
4. Training and education
5. Teamwork
6. Continuous improvement
7. Quality measurement
8. Effective communication
9. Process improvement

Explanation:

- Training: Employees must be trained to perform their work correctly.
- Teamwork: Employees should work together to solve quality problems.

## 6. TQM Cycle (PDCA Cycle)

One of the most important concepts in Total Quality Management is the PDCA cycle.

PDCA stands for:

- P = Plan
- D = Do
- C = Check
- A = Act

It is also called the Deming Cycle or Shewhart Cycle.

### 1. Plan

In this stage, the organization identifies a problem and plans how to improve quality.

Activities:

- Identify the problem.
- Find the reason for the problem.
- Set quality objectives.
- Prepare an improvement plan.

Example: A company identifies that 10% of its electronic circuit boards have soldering defects.

### 2. Do

In this stage, the organization implements the planned solution.

Activities:

- Apply the improvement method.
- Train employees.
- Perform the process.
- Record the results.

Example: The company trains workers in proper soldering techniques and adjusts the soldering temperature.

### 3. Check

In this stage, the results are measured and compared with the planned objectives.

Activities:

- Inspect the products.
- Collect data.
- Measure performance.
- Identify remaining problems.

Example: The company checks whether the number of defective circuit boards has decreased.

### 4. Act

In this stage, the organization takes corrective action based on the results.

Activities:

- Standardize successful improvements.
- Correct remaining problems.
- Update procedures.
- Begin another improvement cycle.

Example: If soldering defects decrease, the company adopts the improved soldering method throughout production.

Important: The PDCA cycle is repeated continuously to achieve better quality.

## 7. Tools and Techniques Used in TQM

There are seven basic quality control tools used to identify, analyze, and solve quality-related problems.



### 1. Check Sheet

A check sheet is a simple form used to collect and record data about defects or problems.

Example:

A company records different defects in electronic circuit boards.

| Defect type       | Number of defects |
| ----------------- | ----------------- |
| Soldering defects | 20                |
| Broken components | 10                |
| PCB damage        | 5                 |
| Loose connections | 15                |

It helps identify the most frequently occurring defects.

### 2. Pareto Chart

A Pareto chart is a bar graph used to identify the major causes of problems.

It follows the 80/20 principle, which suggests that a small number of causes may account for a large percentage of problems.

Example: If most circuit board defects are caused by soldering and loose connections, the company should focus on correcting these problems first.

### 3. Cause-and-Effect Diagram

It is also called the Fishbone Diagram or Ishikawa Diagram.

It is used to identify the possible causes of a particular problem.

Common categories include:

- Man (People)
- Machine
- Method
- Material
- Measurement
- Environment (Mother Nature)

Example: If a PCB has soldering defects, possible causes include:

- Untrained workers.
- Faulty soldering equipment.
- Poor-quality solder.
- Incorrect temperature.
- Improper soldering methods.

## 8. Advantages of Total Quality Management

TQM provides several advantages to an organization.

1. Improves product quality: Produces reliable and high-quality products.
2. Customer satisfaction: Meets customer requirements and expectations.
3. Reduces defects: Minimizes errors during manufacturing.
4. Reduces cost: Reduces wastage, rework, and unnecessary expenses.
5. Increases productivity: Improves the efficiency of employees and machines.
6. Improves teamwork: Encourages employees to work together.
7. Employee motivation: Gives employees opportunities to contribute ideas.
8. Improves company reputation: Builds customer confidence and trust.
9. Better decision making: Uses data and facts to solve problems.
10. Long-term business growth: Helps organizations remain competitive.

## 9. Disadvantages of Total Quality Management

Although TQM provides many benefits, it also has some limitations.

### 1. High Initial Cost

Implementing TQM requires employee training, quality equipment, and process improvements.

### 2. Time-Consuming

Quality improvement requires continuous monitoring, analysis, and evaluation.

### 3. Employee Resistance

Some employees may resist changes in their working methods.

### 4. Requires Management Support

Without strong support from top management, TQM may not be successful.

### 5. Training Requirements

Employees need regular training to understand quality standards and procedures.

### 6. Difficult Implementation

Implementing TQM throughout a large organization can be challenging.

## 11. Difference Between Traditional Quality Control and TQM

| Traditional Quality Control                       | Total Quality Management                          |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Focuses mainly on detecting defects.              | Focuses on preventing defects.                    |
| Quality is mainly the inspector's responsibility. | Quality is everyone's responsibility.             |
| Inspection happens mainly after production.       | Quality is maintained at every stage.             |
| Focuses on short-term results.                    | Focuses on long-term improvement.                 |
| Limited employee involvement.                     | Encourages employee participation.                |
| Corrects problems after they occur.               | Prevents problems through continuous improvement. |

## 12. Real-Time Example of TQM in an Electronics Manufacturing Company

Consider an organization manufacturing electronic circuit boards for industrial applications.

Problem: The company receives complaints because some circuit boards are not working properly.

The company applies TQM as follows:

1. Customer focus: Collects customer complaints and identifies the problems.
2. Quality inspection: Checks the raw materials and electronic components.
3. Employee involvement: Asks technicians and engineers to identify the causes of defects.
4. Process improvement: Improves PCB assembly and soldering methods.
5. Quality testing: Tests every circuit board before delivery.
6. Continuous improvement: Collects test results and improves the manufacturing process.
7. Customer satisfaction: Delivers reliable circuit boards with fewer defects.

Result: Product quality improves, production wastage decreases, and customer satisfaction increases.
